AxLink Settlement Blueprint

How value moves through the network, account by account and leg by leg. The customer deposits their own currency and withdraws their own currency; USD is the unit of account and USDT on Solana is the transport, and neither is ever shown to them. Every settlement is provable against a chain we do not control. This is the architecture as built on the settlement-engine branch, written so it can be implemented exactly.

01

What we are replacing

First, what this is not

This is back-office settlement infrastructure. No customer sees it, and none of it is a product feature. A user deposits their own currency and withdraws their own currency; whether the value moved between our books on a correspondent chain or on Solana is invisible to them, and they have no way to tell. Nothing in this document should ever reach a customer-facing screen.

That invisibility is deliberate and it is worth something: because the customer contract never references the rail, the rail can be replaced without touching the product. A chain outage, a change of settlement asset, a regulator ruling a corridor out — each is an operations decision, not a migration.

So the case for this rail is an internal one — cost per transfer, how fast our own books can be trusted, and what we can hand an auditor. Not a story anyone tells a user.

A cross-border payment on correspondent banking is a chain of bilateral credits between banks that each hold accounts with the next. Nobody in the chain sees the whole path, each hop takes a fee and a cut of the spread, and the sender learns the outcome when it lands — or when it doesn't. Those costs land on us, and we either absorb them or price them into what the customer pays.

AxLink removes the intermediaries by giving both counterparties a position on one shared settlement asset and moving that asset directly. The proof matters to exactly three audiences, and a retail user is not among them: a member bank on the other side of a transfer, an auditor, and us.

Why finality is enforced anyway

None of this reaches a customer, so the discipline has to be justified internally — and it is. No transfer completes on a custodian's acknowledgement, only on a finalized signature. Not because a user asked, but because it is the difference between books we can prove and books we merely believe: a settlement marked complete on an API response is a number nobody can check, including us, until the day someone has to.

02

The four planes

The system separates into four layers that fail independently and must never be confused with one another. Most of the bugs the current codebase carries come from collapsing two of them — treating a custody acknowledgement as settlement, or a balance update as an accounting entry.

Why the separation is load-bearing

  • Ledger ≠ transport. The journal records an obligation; the chain records that value moved. They are reconciled against each other daily, and a disagreement is a finding.
  • Transport ≠ custody. Fireblocks signs. Solana settles. A Fireblocks transaction id proves a request was accepted, not that anything happened.
  • Delivery ≠ settlement. A wire submitted to a converter is not a wire received by a partner. The leg stays AWAITING_CONFIRMATION until the partner says otherwise.
03

The currency model

Three currencies are in play at once and they are constantly confused. Naming them separately is what makes the rest of the system tractable.

Presentation
The customer's own currency. A Kenyan user deposits KES, sees a KES balance, withdraws KES. This is the only currency they ever encounter.
Unit of account
USD. Every internal position, limit and report is measured here. It is the pivot every corridor passes through.
Settlement asset
USDT on Solana. What actually backs the balances and moves between counterparties. Never shown, never sent to a partner.
Which entity holds the asset

The group is licensed differently in each market: FinCEN MSB in the USA, FINTRAC MSB in Canada, the UK entity, and a digital-asset regime in El Salvador. Those are not interchangeable balance sheets. A money-services licence permits moving fiat; it does not by itself permit holding a stablecoin position, and holding one in the wrong entity is a licensing problem rather than an accounting one.

So the USDT position belongs on the balance sheet of the entity licensed to hold digital assets, and the MSB entities hold a fiat receivable from that entity — never the token. Customers are unaffected either way; they see their own currency and never learn which entity settled behind it. But the books have to say it, and today they do not: CUSTODY:* accounts carry no entity dimension. See the gap noted below.

The settlement asset itself is one constant — SETTLEMENT_ASSET plus a mint address in a single table. Switching assets, or running a different one per region, stays a config change. Cheap now, expensive once balances exist.

The converter settles USD, GBP and EUR and nothing else. That single fact determines the shape of every corridor: those three are native, and everything else has to reach USD somehow. Which side needs the bridge is what separates an easy corridor from a hard one.

The asymmetry — and it is not a code problem

Paying out in a local currency is easy: the partner already holds that currency and does the FX for us. Taking money in is the hard direction. The customer's shillings land in a Kenyan collection account, and the converter cannot accept shillings — so a local FX and repatriation partner has to convert KES to USD and wire it out. That is a banking relationship and a capital-controls question in each market, not something the settlement engine can solve. Kenya is workable; Nigeria is materially harder.

Name that partner per market before promising local deposits. Until it exists, Class C corridors cannot run end to end no matter how correct the code is.

Who carries the FX position

If the customer's balance is denominated in KES and the backing is USDT, we are short KES against the customer and long USD — a currency position nobody chose to take. It is small per customer and unbounded in aggregate, and it grows quietly with deposits.

Concretely: a user deposits KES 130,000 and is credited KES 128,700. We hold roughly 995 USDT. If the shilling strengthens from 130 to 120 against the dollar, that USDT is worth KES 119,362 against a KES 128,700 liability — short KES 9,338 on a single customer, with no trade ever placed.

Whichever option you pick, the position has to be measured. Per-currency liability against USD-equivalent backing, reported daily. The journal already carries the data — USER:*:KES summed against custody, both in USD terms — but a report nobody reads is the same as no report. An FX book found during an audit is worse than one that was chosen.

04

Chart of accounts

Every movement has two sides. Before this existed, a withdrawal debited a customer and the money simply left the books — fees, FX spread, partner float and custody had no accounts to land in, so they were visible only as the arithmetic difference between a debit and a payout.

Codes are structured strings, built only through AccountCode.*. A free-form code would open a brand-new account and silently absorb money; an unrecognised prefix raises instead.

Code patternTypeHolds
USER:<userId>:<CCY>LiabilityWhat we owe a customer. Their app balance.
BANK:<bankId>:<CCY>LiabilityA member bank's position on the network.
TREASURY:<CCY>AssetOur own funds.
CUSTODY:<vaultId>:<ASSET>AssetUSDT/USDT held in a named Fireblocks vault.
COLLECTION:<rail>:<CCY>Asset transientLocal currency collected at a rail, not yet repatriated. Class C corridors only.
CONVERTER:<id>:<CCY>Asset transientIn transit at B2BinPay. Should not persist across a close.
FLOAT:<partner>:<CCY>AssetPre-funded balance sitting at a payout partner.
FEE_INCOME:<CCY>IncomeOur revenue.
FX_PNL:<CCY>IncomeConversion clearing. Its net position is the spread.
SUSPENSE:<CCY>Liability transientDebited from a customer, not yet delivered.
Gap — accounts carry no entity

Every code above is group-wide. In a multi-entity licensed group that is not enough: an auditor asks which entity holds a position, and a regulator asks whether that entity is permitted to. The fix is a fourth segment — CUSTODY:sv:treasury:USDT, USER:us:<id>:USD — plus INTERCO:<from>:<to>:<CCY> so positions between group entities are visible instead of netted invisibly into one balance sheet.

Do this before there are balances. Adding an entity dimension to a populated journal means restating history and deciding retroactively which entity held what — the kind of exercise that is straightforward on day one and a project on day two hundred.

Sign convention

Positive is a debit, negative is a credit, in the line's own currency. Every posting sums to zero per currency — not in USD. A cross-currency posting that only balances after conversion is hiding an FX position, so conversions must name FX_PNL explicitly. That is what makes the converter's spread a number on a report instead of an unexplained shortfall.

05

Six primitives

Everything below is composed from these. Learn them once and every scenario reads the same way.

ProviderCapability what a partner can actually do

Declares settlementCurrencies and payoutCurrencies separately, plus methods, countries, limits, and two flags that decide everything else: cryptoLicensed and prefundedFloat.

FundingPlan how this payout gets funded

Resolved before any value moves. Names the settlement currency, the amount, who bears the FX, and — critically — payoutPrecedesFunding, which says whether the beneficiary waits for us.

SettlementLeg one step, written before it is taken

The full plan is persisted in the same transaction as the domain record. Each leg carries a deterministic externalKey handed to the external system as its idempotency key, so a resumed leg reuses the same request instead of creating a second payment.

JournalEntry the books, append-only

Balanced lines, asserted before write, keyed by a deterministic postingKey. Re-posting is a no-op — which is what makes the resume worker safe to run repeatedly. A correction is a reversing entry, never an edit.

SettlementProof evidence, not assertion

Signature, slot, block time, mint, amount and fee actually paid — recorded only after an RPC node reports finalized and the on-chain movement matches what we intended.

IdempotencyKey pay once

Arbitrated by a unique constraint, not a read-then-write check — two concurrent requests both pass a read-then-write check. Same key with a different body is rejected, never served the cached response.

How a partner gets funded

This is the decision that makes corridors pluggable. It runs before any value moves, so a corridor with no viable route fails while the money is still ours — rather than after USDT has been moved to a converter that cannot produce the payout currency.

Which mode a corridor lands in

ModePartner funded inBeneficiary waits for our wire?Capital cost
ATOMIC_USDTUSDTNoNone
PREFUNDED_FLOATUSD (replenishment)No — paid from floatFloat size, per corridor
DIRECT_FIATPayout currencyYesNone
PARTNER_FXUSD / EUR / GBPYesNone
The trade you have to make

Our payout partners cannot hold crypto, which rules out ATOMIC_USDT. That leaves the two goals written throughout this system in direct tension: zero pre-funded corridors and real-time execution. You can have either, not both. Without float, the beneficiary cannot be paid until our fiat wire lands at the partner — banking rails, banking hours. PREFUNDED_FLOAT buys real-time for the recipient at the cost of working capital parked in each corridor, bounded by the float size. Both are implemented; it is a per-partner config flag, not a code change.

07

The leg state machine

A settlement is a sequence of legs, each of which either happened or did not. Persisting the plan before executing it is what makes a crash between the customer debit and the partner payout recoverable instead of permanent.

Check the books

Assets 9,985.00 USDT. Liabilities 9,975.00 to the customer. Income: +25.00 fee, −15.00 FX loss (the converter's spread, now sitting visibly in FX_PNL) = +10.00. Assets minus liabilities equals retained earnings: 9,985 − 9,975 = 10. The customer's balance is over-collateralised by $10, and you can see exactly why. Under the old single-entry ledger this $15 spread had nowhere to land and would have appeared as an unexplained shortfall.

The RPC node is unreachable mid-settlement

failure

USDT has been moved at the custodian. We go to verify the signature and the Solana node times out. Three outcomes must stay distinct here, and conflating them loses money.

What we observeWhat it meansWhat the system does
Signature finalized, no errorSettledRecord SettlementProof, leg → COMPLETED
Signature landed, on-chain errorDid not settleSettlementNotFinalError, proof row records the failure, leg → FAILED
Cannot reach any nodeNothing at allPROOF_UNAVAILABLE, retryable, leg stays AWAITING_CONFIRMATION
Never treat a timeout as a failure

A timeout says the call did not answer in time, not that it did not happen. Reversing a transfer because an RPC node was slow reverses a transfer that may well have settled. The leg holds, the resume worker polls, and if it never resolves it surfaces in /api/settlement/stuck for a person.

The practical version of this failure is mundane: SOLANA_RPC_URL left pointing at the public mainnet endpoint. It rate-limits under load, verification starts failing, and because the engine correctly refuses to complete unproven transfers, the visible symptom is settlements piling up. Use a dedicated node.

What the old code did

Credited the gross amount back and moved on. The custody leg was never reversed, the fee was silently retained with no entry, and the conversion cost had nowhere to land — three separate holes in the books from one failed payout.

The client retries and sends the request twice

failure

A mobile client times out and resubmits. Two identical withdrawal requests arrive concurrently, milliseconds apart, on two application instances. 

// Both callers race a unique constraint. Exactly one wins the insert.
prisma.idempotencyKey.create({ data: { scope: 'withdrawal', key, requestHash, status: 'IN_FLIGHT' } })

// Winner  → runs the work, stores the response, returns EXECUTED
// Loser   → P2002 violation → routed to the replay path
//   same requestHash + COMPLETED  → REPLAYED, returns the stored response
//   same requestHash + IN_FLIGHT  → 409 REQUEST_IN_FLIGHT
//   different requestHash         → 409 IDEMPOTENCY_CONFLICT — never the cached response
Why not a check
The old checkIdempotency read for an existing row and then wrote. Two concurrent callers both read nothing, both passed, and both settled — and there was no unique constraint behind it to catch them.
Stale in-flight
A row held longer than 5 minutes means the process holding it is gone. A retry takes it over. Five minutes comfortably exceeds every partner timeout we set.
Changed body
Rejected outright. Reusing a reference with different parameters is a client bug, and serving the first payment's response to the second request would hide it.

Adding KES via M-Pesa — no core changes

extension

The scenario above shows the money moving. This shows the code that makes it possible. Safaricom is an MNO with a mobile-money licence, not a VASP: it cannot receive crypto, and the converter cannot produce KES. Under the old atomicJit: boolean this corridor had no representation at all and threw — which is why the entire Africa book did not work. 

// The whole integration. No changes to the orchestrator, the ledger, or the engine.
readonly capability = {
  settlementCurrencies: ['USD'],           // fund us in dollars
  payoutCurrencies:     ['KES', 'TZS', 'UGX'],
  methods:              ['mobile_money'],
  countries:            ['KE', 'TZ', 'UG'],
  partnerAppliesFx:     true,              // they convert USD → KES at their rate
  cryptoLicensed:       false,             // hard block on any USDT plan
  prefundedFloat:       false,             // flip to true when a float is funded
};

The resolver reaches step 4 and returns PARTNER_FX: fund in USD, size the wire from an indicative USD/KES rate, and mark fxBearer: 'PARTNER' so the delivered amount is understood to be confirmed by Safaricom rather than promised by us.

The point of the abstraction

A new corridor is a capability declaration and a provider class. It touches no shared code path, so it cannot break USD. That is the test of whether "plug in any payout partner" is real or just an interface with the word interface in front of it.

Proof
Both banks fetch GET /api/settlement/proof/InterbankTransfer/<id>. It is unauthenticated on purpose — a proof only we can read proves nothing. The response carries the signature, slot and an explorer link they can check on a node neither of us runs.
Notification
Written to the outbox in the same transaction as the settlement, HMAC-signed, delivered at-least-once with backoff. Receivers dedupe on trackingId. Limits
Daily cap per bank and per-corridor caps, checked before the transfer is created.
09

Invariants

These are the properties the system is built to preserve. Each one has a check that enforces it and a place it surfaces when it breaks. An invariant with no check is a wish.

  • I-1Every posting balances to zero, per currency. Asserted in assertBalanced before write. A break in the daily trial balance means a write bypassed the journal — reported CRITICAL, because it invalidates every other number.
  • I-2No transfer completes without a finalized signature. Reconciliation flags any COMPLETED withdrawal with no SettlementProof. Proving against a stub custody id is refused outright.
  • I-3Suspense is zero at close of day. A non-zero balance is a customer debited and not delivered. This is an incident, not a reconciling item.
  • I-4Account.balance equals the journal. Both written in one transaction. The projection-drift check is the tripwire for a legacy debit/credit call that skipped the journal.
  • I-5One idempotency key pays once. Enforced by a unique constraint, not application logic.
  • I-6No crypto reaches an unlicensed partner. Enforced in the funding resolver, which fails closed when cryptoLicensed is merely unset rather than assuming.
  • I-7Our books agree with custody, and custody agrees with the chain. Two separate checks — that is what tells you whether a discrepancy is ours or the custodian's.
  • I-8/settlement/stuck is empty each morning. Every row is money in an indeterminate state that retrying will not fix.
  • I-9The FX position is measured daily, per currency. Local-currency liabilities against USD-equivalent backing. A position that is small today and unmeasured is an FX book found during an audit rather than one that was chosen.

Trust model

Worth being explicit about, because "trustless" is usually overclaimed. Here is what each party must actually take on faith, and what they can check for themselves.

PartyMust trustCan verify independently
CustomerThat we hold their balance and will honour itTheir statement, in their own currency. They see no rail, no asset and no proof — and would not know if the rail changed.
Member bankOur FX rate and fee at quote timeThat settlement happened — signature, slot, amount, on any node
Payout partnerThat our wire will arriveTheir own statement against our ProviderAttempt log
RegulatorNothing structuralTrial balance, journal, proofs, reconciliation history
UsCustodian signs what we asked; converter honours its rateBoth — against the chain, and against the journal

The honest summary: settlement is verifiable, custody is not. We still trust Fireblocks to sign correctly and B2BinPay to convert at the quoted rate. What we no longer do is trust either of them about whether value actually moved — that question is answered by a chain, and the answer is recorded whether it agrees with them or not.

Rollout

Genuinely sequential — each phase depends on the one before it being true in production, not merely deployed.

Phase 1 · before any live money

Baseline the schema and prove the books

  • Run scripts/create-baseline-migration.sh and commit prisma/migrations/. Until this exists, prisma migrate deploy silently does nothing and a deploy succeeds against an unmigrated database.
  • Point SOLANA_RPC_URL at a dedicated node.
  • Run a full reconciliation on an empty database. It must come back CLEAN.
  • Decide the entity dimension now. Which entity holds the USDT position, and which hold fiat receivables against it. Retrofitting this onto a populated journal means restating history.
Phase 2 · sandbox, no customer funds

Walk the USD corridor end to end

  • Deposit, then withdrawal, against sandbox credentials. Check the trial balance after each.
  • Kill the process mid-settlement and confirm the resume worker completes it.
  • Submit a duplicate withdrawal and confirm exactly one payout.
  • Point at a bad RPC URL and confirm settlements hold rather than fail.
Phase 3 · live, capped

USD only, one partner, low limits

  • JIT wire mode. Not real-time for the recipient, and the product copy must say so.
  • Daily reconciliation, reviewed by a person. Suspense checked at close.
  • Cap per transaction and per day until a month of clean reconciliations.
Phase 4 · the real-time decision

Fund a float, if the working capital case holds

  • Size the float to expected daily volume. Flip prefundedFloat on that partner alone.
  • Monitor FLOAT:* as a first-class balance — a failed replenishment is now a treasury exposure, not a customer-facing failure.
Phase 5 · expansion

KES, then NGN

  • Withdrawals first. Paying out in local currency is Class B and works today — a capability declaration plus a provider class, no core changes. If a corridor needs a core change, the abstraction is wrong and should be fixed rather than worked around.
  • Local deposits are gated on a repatriation partner. Class C cannot run until someone is contracted to convert KES to USD and wire it out. Name them, and settle the capital-controls question, before the feature is promised.
  • Settle the currency-model decision before the first local deposit lands, not after. Once local-denominated balances exist, the FX position exists.
  • Watch FX_PNL per corridor: under PARTNER_FX the partner sets the rate, so this is where a bad partner rate becomes visible instead of absorbed.
12

Not yet

Deliberately out of v1, recorded so they are decisions rather than oversights.

Netting

Every transfer currently settles gross — one on-chain hop per payment. At 5,000 lamports that is survivable, but it forfeits the main structural advantage of running your own network: a thousand transfers between two member banks in a window should net to one settlement leg, and per-transfer confirmation latency should come off the critical path. The schema was drafted and then removed rather than shipping unused tables. It becomes worthwhile at the point where two member banks have real bilateral volume, which is a Phase 5 conversation.

Migrating the legacy ledger helpers

debit and credit in ledger.service.ts still mutate balances without posting. The USD corridor does not use them; older services do. The projection-drift check catches any that remain, which is the safe order — detect first, migrate second.

Decimal handling in the older services

Decimal(20,8) columns are still read into JavaScript number across the pre-existing code, reintroducing exactly the float error the column type was chosen to avoid. The settlement engine uses Prisma.Decimal throughout and never converts.
